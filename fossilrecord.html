Fossil Record

The fossils were first found and recorded in 1867 by Jay Terrell, who was a owner of a hotel and an amateur palentologist who mainly collected fossils in the cliffs that reside along Lake Erie located near his home of Sheffield Lake, Ohio, United States. After discovering these fossils, Terrell donated his fossils to John Strong Newberry and the Ohio Geological Survey.

The species had been claimed to be a new genus and species not until 1873 and named the Dinichthys herzeri.

Pictured here is Paleontologist Peter Bungart, a protege of Jay Terrell, with the skull of Dunkleosteus terrelli that he assembled at the Cleveland Museum of Natural History.

Currently the largest collection of Dunkleosteus fossils resides in the Cleveland Museum of Natural History.

To continue, Dunkleosteus fossils have been found in multiple regions which include Poland, Belgium, and Morocco. These regions used to be a shallow tropical sea, which explains the wide distribution of these fossils.

The fossils being preserved only consists of it's heavily armored head. The head consists of skull plates, jaw bones, and Thoracic armor. This is speculated to be the reason it's usually only the head because it's more likely to fossil since the rear body mainly consists of tissue which always never gets preserved.



Scientists have unfortunately never found a complete skeleton, with it's body reconstruction mainly being referenced from other related species that are similar.
